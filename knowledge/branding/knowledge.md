# Branding knowledge

- 2.2.0 replaced the SF Symbol `eye.fill` logo (the user disliked it) with a zoned-out face drawn in CoreGraphics: a black disc, yellow half-lidded eyes, a flat mouth, and a drool drop. Candidates were spiral "hypnotized" eyes and X eyes; the zoned face read best at 16 px.
- The 16 px icon drops the mouth and drool and scales the face up 1.3x; below that size the features merge.
- 2.3.0 swapped the zoned face for a woozy one after the user asked for something more "woozy". It follows the 🥴 emoji cues: one droopy half-open eye, one squeezed `<` eye pointing at the nose, coral flushed cheeks, a wavy mouth, and the whole face tilted about 8° counterclockwise. At 16 px it keeps only the two eyes.
- A droopy eye whose lid slopes down toward the nose reads as angry, not woozy. The lid must slope down toward the outside.
- Fonts: Gluten (Etcetera Type Co, OFL, variable weight and slant) for the wordmark, counts, overlay and pill; Fredoka (OFL, rounded) for UI text. They are bundled as latin woff2 in `fonts/`. Content scripts load Gluten through `chrome-extension://__MSG_@@extension_id__/fonts/…` and need `web_accessible_resources`.
- `icons/logo.svg` mirrors the Swift geometry in a 24x24 y-down grid. Keep the two in sync when changing the face.
- The popup uses a yellow gradient hero with a big total and an ink progress meter, white cards for everything else, and a segmented control for mode.
- The user found the first 2.3.0 woozy styling (blush, tilted face, Gluten/Fredoka, rotated UI elements) too gimmicky and asked for something more professional. The revision keeps the woozy face but drops the blush and tilt and flattens the mouth wave. It uses Inter everywhere with no rotations, and a flat yellow pill.
- The user then asked for an icon that was more professional still, but kept the woozy reference. Candidates were the disc face, a monoline version and a flat version (ink features straight on the yellow tile, no disc). Flat won: it reads as a modern app mark and still reads at 16px when the features are scaled up 1.12x. The tile went from gradient to solid `#FFD100`.
- Platform marks come from Bootstrap Icons (MIT): `linkedin`, `twitter-x`, `youtube`. Simple Icons dropped LinkedIn after a brand request, so it was not an option.
- The popup count tiles show only the platform logos (with `title` and `aria-label`), not the text names.
- The popup was restyled after a user-supplied reference (a "Nodera" B2B CRM dashboard shot). It uses a warm grey canvas `#efece8`, white 22px-radius cards with no borders, and one dark card (the hero). The palette is black/yellow/red. Pill buttons and a black pill for the active segment; thin red share bars per blocked category; big numbers at weight 500 in card headers; and a red gradient call-out card for "Cost of today's scroll". On-page labels are white pills with a red dot and a black Show button, and the cost is a yellow chip.
- 2026-09-30: the user rejected the Nodera-style popup ("i don't like the ui at all"). Five directions were mocked in `design/directions.html` with screenshots in `design/shots/`. A, Receipt: mono type, a till receipt, voided posts, cost as the total. B, Instrument: a hardware panel with a lit display, keycaps and a fader. C, Coach: serif sentences with fill-in-the-blank settings. D, Log: dark tabs, an hourly chart and a list of hidden posts you can reopen; blocked posts collapse to one line. E, Poster: a glance at one huge number on yellow, a "Stop now" action and a separate settings page.
- The user picked C, Coach. It is built into the real popup. The popup opens with a serif lead sentence (Source Serif 4, bundled as `fonts/source-serif-latin.woff2`) whose wording changes for 0 posts, under the limit ("about two thirds of your limit"), over the limit, and wind down. Below it: a 10-dot meter, a platforms sentence using logos only, a Jev sentence, and a wind-down countdown with a conic clock. Settings are a fill-in-the-blank sentence with inline tokens; mode radios and category checkboxes are styled as tokens. Help text only shows lock reasons or warnings. Blocked posts get a white card with one sentence (`pillSentence`), the cost, and a "Show anyway" link.
- Chrome's `field-sizing: content` lets inline inputs inside a sentence fit their value.
